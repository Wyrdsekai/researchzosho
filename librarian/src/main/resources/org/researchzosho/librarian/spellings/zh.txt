# Chinese names in Latin letters.

# A Chinese name is written in pinyin (Zhang Wei, Deng Xiaoping) since 1979, and in Wade-Giles before it and in Taiwan (Chang Wei, Teng
# Hsiao-p'ing), often without its apostrophes and umlauts; families of the south and of Southeast Asia write their family names as their
# dialect says them (Cheung, Chan, Wong, Ng, Lim). A name in characters is read by a source or the model, never by these rules: Chinese
# characters are Japanese as often, and the program takes a name in characters with no other sign as Japanese. These rules compare and
# search the Latin forms of a Chinese name.
#
# Each syllable line gives the pinyin and the Wade-Giles. Wade-Giles without its apostrophes writes several pinyin syllables one way
# (chang is pinyin zhang and chang; chi is ji and qi), so they compare as one: lossy, as the records themselves are.
